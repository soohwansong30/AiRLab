Physical AI

We study how robots can connect visual observations and language instructions to actions in the physical world. Our research brings together predictive world models, Vision-Language-Action models, and spatially grounded planning to make robot behavior more adaptable to changing tasks and environments.

Vision-Language-Action & Robot World Models

Ongoing research

Robots need more than object recognition to perform a task: they must relate an instruction to the current scene and anticipate how their actions may change it. We investigate Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models alongside robot world models to connect perception, prediction, and action selection.

  • Visual and language grounding: Relate task instructions to objects, the robot, and its end effector using visual observations.
  • Predictive scene modeling: Study how learned models can generate plausible future observations and represent changes associated with robot actions.
  • Action selection and feedback: Explore how predictions can inform robot actions and how new observations can guide subsequent decisions.

3D Vision-Language-Planning

Ongoing research project, 2026-2029

Moving an object between locations requires a robot team to understand where objects are, which robot can perform each step, and how those steps depend on one another. Our 3D Vision-Language-Planning (3D VLP) project investigates a shared spatial representation that supports this reasoning across heterogeneous robots.

  • Semantic 3D representation: Integrate visual information from multiple robots into a map that connects scene geometry with object and place semantics.
  • Spatially grounded task planning: Interpret language instructions in the context of the map and decompose them into actions suited to different robot capabilities.
  • Collaborative execution and validation: Investigate how plans can be coordinated, updated during execution, and evaluated through simulation and real-robot experiments.
